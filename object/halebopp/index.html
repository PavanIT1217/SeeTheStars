Comet Hale–Bopp

Comet · The Great Comet of 1997

Hale–Bopp was one of the most-watched comets in history. It was so big and bright that it could be seen even from city streets, and it stayed visible to the naked eye for about a year and a half – a record.

Take a closer look

🔭

Spotted from afar

When Hale–Bopp was discovered in 1995, it was still out beyond the orbit of Jupiter – much farther away than comets are usually found. Being visible from so far away was the first clue that its icy nucleus was enormous, several times wider than Halley’s.

✨

A secret third tail

Besides its famous dust and gas tails, Hale–Bopp had a faint third tail that nobody had seen on a comet before. It was made of sodium – the same element that makes old street lamps glow orange – and astronomers found it using special filters on their telescopes.

🪐

A tug from Jupiter

In 1996, on its way towards the Sun, Hale–Bopp passed fairly close to Jupiter. The giant planet’s gravity tugged on it and changed its path, shortening its trip around the Sun from more than 4,000 years to about 2,400. Planets can steer comets like this.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

Hale–Bopp’s last visit before 1997 was more than 4,000 years ago – a few centuries after the Great Pyramid of Giza was built.

How we found out

  1. 1995

    On the same night, 23 July, Alan Hale in New Mexico and Thomas Bopp in Arizona independently discover it.

  2. 1996–97

    It becomes visible without a telescope and stays that way for about 18 months.

  3. 1997

    It passes closest to the Sun on 1 April, showing off two bright tails.

  4. Today

    It is heading back into the cold outer Solar System. Large telescopes still track it.

🤔 Something to wonder about

Why do some comets have two tails?

One tail is dust, pushed gently by sunlight into a curved, yellowish fan. The other is gas, blown straight back by the solar wind and glowing blue.

See it yourself

Not any more – it is far beyond Neptune, deep in the southern sky.
