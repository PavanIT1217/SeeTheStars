Orion Nebula (M42)

Nebula · Star nursery

The Orion Nebula is a giant glowing cloud of gas and dust where thousands of new stars are being born. It is the closest large star-forming region to Earth and you can see it without a telescope.

Take a closer look

💠

The Trapezium’s glow

At the nebula’s heart, a small telescope shows four bright stars in a tight little shape called the Trapezium. These young stars are fiercely hot. Their ultraviolet light makes the surrounding gas glow and has carved out a hollow in the cloud, like a cave lit from inside.

🧊

Tip of the cloud

The glowing part is only a bright blister on the near side of a far bigger, cold, dark cloud. Hidden behind it, more stars are forming in thick dust. Infrared telescopes like Webb can see through that dust to find them.

☀️

Our Sun’s baby pictures

Our Sun was probably born in a crowded star nursery much like this one, about 4.6 billion years ago. Studying the Orion Nebula helps scientists picture how our own Solar System began. Its pink colour comes from glowing hydrogen – the same gas that fuels the Sun today.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

The cloud contains enough gas to make about 2,000 stars like the Sun.

How we found out

  1. 1610

    Nicolas-Claude Fabri de Peiresc makes the first recorded telescope observation of the nebula.

  2. 1769

    Charles Messier adds it to his catalogue as M42.

  3. 1880

    Henry Draper takes the first-ever photograph of a nebula – this one.

  4. 1990s

    Hubble discovers dusty discs around young stars – baby solar systems in the making.

🤔 Something to wonder about

How does a cloud of gas become a star?

Gravity slowly pulls clumps of gas together. As a clump shrinks, it heats up until its centre is hot enough for nuclear fusion – and a star switches on.

⏳ Light is a time machine

The light reaching you now set off about 1,344 years ago – around the time Aryabhata wrote that the Earth spins.

See it yourself

Look at the “sword” hanging below Orion’s belt. The middle “star” looks fuzzy – that’s the nebula. Binoculars make it glow.
