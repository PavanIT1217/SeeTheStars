Makemake

Dwarf planet · Kuiper Belt

Makemake is a reddish-brown dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt, covered in frozen methane and ethane. It is one of the brightest objects out there, but still far too faint to see without a big telescope.

⭐

A star that blinked

In 2011, Makemake passed in front of a faint star, and astronomers watched closely. The star vanished and reappeared suddenly instead of fading slowly, which showed that Makemake has little or no global atmosphere. That makes it different from Pluto, which wraps itself in thin haze.

⚖️

Weighing with a moon

Makemake’s small dark moon is a useful tool. By measuring how long a moon takes to circle its parent world and how far away it is, scientists can work out the parent’s mass. Once they pin down this moon’s orbit, they will be able to weigh Makemake.

🥶

Far, far away

Makemake is currently more than 50 times further from the Sun than Earth is, so sunlight takes around seven hours to reach it. Its surface is colder than −230 °C, so cold that methane – a gas on Earth – lies frozen on the ground like frost.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

Its moon is so dark it reflects about as much light as charcoal.

Size it up

You could line up 9 of them across Earth. Both are drawn to the same scale.

How we found out

  1. 2005

    Discovered on 31 March by Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz, just after Easter – the team nicknamed it “Easterbunny”.

  2. 2008

    Officially named after Makemake, the creator god of the people of Rapa Nui (Easter Island).

  3. 2016

    Hubble discovers a tiny, very dark moon nicknamed “MK2”.

🤔 Something to wonder about

Why are distant icy worlds often reddish?

Sunlight and cosmic rays slowly change methane ice into dark reddish compounds called tholins.
