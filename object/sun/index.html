The Sun

Star · Yellow dwarf

The Sun is a star – a giant ball of hot gas. In its core, hydrogen atoms smash together to make helium, releasing the energy that lights and warms our planet. It holds about 99.8% of all the mass in the Solar System.

Take a closer look

🔥

Not a campfire

The Sun isn’t burning like a fire – there is no oxygen to burn. It is made of about three-quarters hydrogen and one-quarter helium, so hot that the gas becomes glowing plasma. Every second, its core turns about 4 million tonnes of matter into pure energy, and it has enough fuel left for billions of years.

🌀

Spots and storms

Sunspots are darker patches where tangled magnetic fields hold back the heat, and many are bigger than Earth. Their numbers rise and fall in a cycle of about 11 years. Near the busy peak, the Sun hurls out flares and giant clouds of gas that can light up auroras on Earth.

💨

A wind from the Sun

The Sun constantly blows a stream of charged particles into space called the solar wind, racing outward at over a million kilometres per hour. It inflates an enormous bubble around all the planets, called the heliosphere. Voyager 1, launched in 1977, crossed its edge in 2012 and entered interstellar space.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

The energy that reaches you as sunlight today was made in the Sun’s core tens of thousands of years ago; it takes that long to bounce its way out to the surface.

Size it up

About 109 Earths would fit side by side across it. Both are drawn to the same scale.

How we found out

  1. 1543

    Copernicus puts the Sun at the centre of the Solar System.

  2. 1610s

    Galileo and others observe sunspots, showing the Sun changes and rotates.

  3. 1868

    Astronomers discover a new element in sunlight and name it helium, after Helios, the Greek Sun god. It is found on Earth only in 1895.

  4. 1939

    Hans Bethe explains how fusion powers the stars.

  5. 2024

    NASA’s Parker Solar Probe flies just 6.1 million km from the Sun’s surface – the closest any human-made object has ever been.

🤔 Something to wonder about

What will happen when the Sun runs out of fuel?

In about 5 billion years it will swell into a red giant, then puff off its outer layers and shrink into a white dwarf about the size of Earth.

See it yourself

⚠️ NEVER look directly at the Sun, and never through binoculars or a telescope – it can blind you instantly. Use certified eclipse glasses or a pinhole projector.
