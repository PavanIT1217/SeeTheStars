3I/ATLAS

Interstellar comet · The third visitor

3I/ATLAS is the third known object from another star system, and the fastest. It is an active comet, growing a tail as it passed the Sun. Some astronomers think it could be billions of years older than our Solar System.

Take a closer look

🚀

A cosmic speed record

3I/ATLAS entered our Solar System at about 58 kilometres every second – fast enough to cross from London to New York in under two minutes. It came from the direction of Sagittarius, towards the crowded centre of our Milky Way galaxy.

🧊

Alien ice

The James Webb Space Telescope found that the gas streaming from 3I/ATLAS is full of carbon dioxide – much more, compared with water, than in most comets from our own Solar System. Clues like this tell scientists about the cold place where it formed, around a star we will never see.

🌞

Hiding behind the Sun

When 3I/ATLAS made its closest pass by the Sun, it was on the far side of the Sun from Earth, so telescopes here could not watch. It never came near us: it always stayed more than 250 million kilometres from Earth, much farther away than the Sun.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

It is moving so fast that the Sun’s gravity can’t hold on to it. After this visit, it will never come back.

How we found out

  1. 2019

    Amateur astronomer Gennadiy Borisov discovers 2I/Borisov, the first interstellar comet with a clear tail.

  2. 2025

    On 1 July, the ATLAS survey telescope in Chile discovers a third interstellar visitor.

  3. 2025

    In October it passes Mars, and spacecraft orbiting Mars photograph it. Later that month it makes its closest approach to the Sun.

  4. 2025–26

    Telescopes around the world and in space study it as it heads back out into interstellar space.

🤔 Something to wonder about

How many interstellar visitors are there?

Probably lots – most are just too small and faint to spot. The new Vera C. Rubin Observatory is expected to find many more.

See it yourself

Large telescopes only, and it is fading as it leaves.
