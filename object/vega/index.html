Vega

Star · White main-sequence

Vega is one of the brightest stars in the sky and part of the “Summer Triangle”. It spins so fast that it bulges at its equator. Astronomers used it as the standard for measuring how bright other stars are.

Take a closer look

🌀

A twelve-hour day

Vega spins all the way round in about 12 and a half hours – our Sun takes nearly a month. The spin flings its middle outwards, so its equator bulges and is cooler and dimmer than its poles. From Earth we see Vega almost pole-on, like looking down at a spinning top.

🌱

A young star

Vega is only about 450 million years old – a tenth of the Sun’s age. It is about twice as heavy as the Sun, so it burns its fuel faster and will live only around a billion years in all. When dinosaurs walked on Earth, Vega was already shining.

💿

A puzzling smooth disc

Vega is circled by a wide disc of dust, a bit like a giant version of the icy belt beyond Neptune. When the Hubble and Webb telescopes looked closely, the disc turned out to be surprisingly smooth, with no clear gaps carved by big planets. Scientists are still puzzling over why.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

About 12,000 years ago, Vega was the North Star – and it will be again in about 12,000 years.

How we found out

  1. 1850

    Vega becomes the first star (after the Sun) ever photographed, at Harvard College Observatory.

  2. 1872

    Henry Draper takes the first photograph of a star’s spectrum – Vega’s.

  3. 1983

    The IRAS space telescope finds a disc of dust around Vega, a hint that planets could be forming there.

🤔 Something to wonder about

In the movie “Contact”, a message comes from Vega. Could aliens live there?

Vega is young and spins fast; any planets would be very different from Earth. But the question of life elsewhere is one of science’s biggest – nobody knows yet!

⏳ Light is a time machine

The light reaching you now set off 25 years ago – maybe before you were born!
