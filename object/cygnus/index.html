Cygnus

Constellation · The Swan

Cygnus is a swan flying along the Milky Way. Its brightest star, Deneb, together with Vega and Altair, forms the Summer Triangle. The Kepler space telescope stared at a patch of sky near Cygnus to discover thousands of planets around other stars.

Take a closer look

💡

Deneb, the far-off beacon

Vega and Altair, the other two stars of the Summer Triangle, are both within about 25 light-years of us. Deneb is well over a thousand light-years away, yet it still shines brightly in our sky. To manage that, it must shine tens of thousands of times more strongly than the Sun.

📐

The first star measured

In 1838 Friedrich Bessel measured the distance to a faint star in Cygnus called 61 Cygni – the first star other than the Sun ever measured. He tracked how it shifts slightly as Earth moves around the Sun. It turned out to be about 11 light-years away.

🗺️

A continent in the sky

Near Deneb glows the North America Nebula, a cloud of gas shaped remarkably like the continent, with dark dust forming its “Gulf of Mexico”. Running through Cygnus is the Great Rift, a lane of dark dust that seems to split the Milky Way in two.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

The star Albireo, at the swan’s head, is actually two stars – one gold and one blue. A small telescope shows them beautifully.

How we found out

  1. Ancient Greece

    In one story the swan is the god Zeus in disguise.

  2. 1964

    Cygnus X-1 is discovered – later the first object widely accepted to be a black hole.

  3. 2009–2013

    NASA’s Kepler telescope watches about 150,000 stars in Cygnus and Lyra and finds thousands of exoplanets.

🤔 Something to wonder about

How can you find a planet around another star if you can’t see it?

Kepler looked for tiny dips in a star’s brightness when a planet passed in front of it – like a moth flying past a distant streetlight.

See it yourself

High overhead on northern summer evenings. Look for a long cross shape along the Milky Way.
