Kepler-186f

Rocky planet · First Earth-sized world in a habitable zone

Kepler-186f was the first Earth-sized planet discovered in the habitable zone of another star. It gets about a third of the energy Earth gets from the Sun, so if it has water, it may be cold – but not necessarily frozen.

Take a closer look

🌅

Noon like sunset

Kepler-186f’s star is a red dwarf about half the size of the Sun. Standing on the planet at midday, its light would look about as bright as sunlight here an hour before sunset. Everything would be lit by a warm, orangey glow, all day long.

⚖️

Size known, weight unknown

Kepler measured the planet’s size from the tiny dip in starlight each time it passed in front of its star. But the star is too faint to measure its wobble, so we don’t know the planet’s mass. Without that, scientists can’t be certain it is made of rock like Earth.

🕰️

Old light

Kepler-186f is about 580 light-years away, so the light Kepler saw had been travelling for nearly six centuries. It set out in the 1400s, around the time the printing press was invented in Europe. We see this star system as it was back then, not as it is now.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

If plants grow there, scientists have suggested they might be dark red or even black, to soak up the reddish light of their star.

How we found out

  1. 2009

    NASA launches the Kepler space telescope to stare at one patch of sky in Cygnus and Lyra.

  2. 2014

    Elisa Quintana’s team announces Kepler-186f, the outermost of five planets around its star.

  3. 2018

    Kepler retires after finding more than 2,600 confirmed planets.

🤔 Something to wonder about

What makes a planet a good home for life?

Liquid water, a protective atmosphere, the right temperature and probably a magnetic field. Being Earth-sized helps – but it doesn’t guarantee any of these.

⏳ Light is a time machine

The light reaching you now set off about 580 years ago – around the time Columbus crossed the Atlantic.

See it yourself

Only with large telescopes. It lies in Cygnus, the Swan.
