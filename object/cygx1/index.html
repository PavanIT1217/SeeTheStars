Cygnus X-1

Black hole · Stellar-mass

Cygnus X-1 is a black hole pulling gas off a giant blue companion star. As the gas spirals in, it heats up to millions of degrees and glows in X-rays. It was the first object most astronomers agreed was a black hole.

Take a closer look

🚗

How big is it?

Cygnus X-1 packs about 21 Suns’ worth of matter into a dark sphere only around 120 km across. You could drive across that distance on a motorway in about an hour. Anything that crosses that edge, the event horizon, can never come back out.

🌬️

Feeding on a star’s wind

Its partner is a blue supergiant around 40 times heavier than the Sun. The two orbit closer together than Mercury is to the Sun. The giant star blows off a strong wind of gas, and the black hole gobbles some of it, forming a hot, swirling disc.

⚡

The telltale flicker

The X-rays from Cygnus X-1 flicker in less than a hundredth of a second. Something can’t flicker faster than light can cross it, so the glowing region must be tiny. That clue, plus its large mass, convinced astronomers this was a black hole.

✨ Mind-blowing fact

Hawking bet against black holes as an “insurance policy”: if all his work on black holes turned out wrong, at least he’d win the bet.

How we found out

  1. 1964

    Discovered by X-ray detectors on a rocket flight.

  2. 1971–72

    Astronomers find its companion star and work out the hidden object is too heavy to be a neutron star.

  3. 1974

    Stephen Hawking bets physicist Kip Thorne that Cygnus X-1 is NOT a black hole – hoping to lose.

  4. 1990

    Hawking concedes the bet.

🤔 Something to wonder about

If black holes don’t let light escape, how can we see them?

We see what happens around them: gas heating up as it falls in, and stars being pulled into orbits around “nothing”.

⏳ Light is a time machine

The light reaching you now set off about 7,200 years ago – a while before people first invented writing.

See it yourself

Near the star Eta Cygni in the Swan’s neck. You can see the companion star with binoculars – but not the black hole!
