Key facts

The Milky Way
The Milky Way is our home galaxy – a huge spinning disc of stars, gas and dust with a bar in the middle and spiral arms. The Sun is in one of the arms. The pale band of light across a dark sky is the galaxy seen edge-on from inside.
Large Magellanic Cloud
The Large Magellanic Cloud is a small galaxy orbiting our own. To the eye it looks like a detached piece of the Milky Way. It contains the Tarantula Nebula, the most active star-forming region in our neighbourhood of galaxies.
Small Magellanic Cloud
The Small Magellanic Cloud is a dwarf galaxy orbiting the Milky Way. It is famous because studying its stars gave astronomers a way to measure the size of the universe.
Andromeda Galaxy (M31)
The Andromeda Galaxy is the nearest large galaxy to the Milky Way – a giant spiral with about a trillion stars. It is the most distant thing most people can see with their own eyes.
Triangulum Galaxy (M33)
The Triangulum Galaxy is the third-largest galaxy in our Local Group, after Andromeda and the Milky Way. It is a beautiful, loosely wound spiral, full of pink star-forming regions.
Whirlpool Galaxy (M51)
The Whirlpool Galaxy is a classic spiral seen face-on, tugged by a smaller companion galaxy. Their gravitational dance has sharpened its spiral arms and triggered the birth of many new stars.
Sombrero Galaxy (M104)
The Sombrero Galaxy has a brilliant bulge of stars and a dark lane of dust around its edge, making it look like a wide-brimmed hat. It was one of the galaxies that revealed the universe is expanding.
